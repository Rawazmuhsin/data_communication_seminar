01 / The Foundation
mMTC — Massive Machine-Type Communications
mMTC is one of three ITU-R M.2083 service categories in 5G, alongside eMBB and URLLC. Its design goal is connection density — not throughput or latency. It supports up to 1,000,000 devices per km² transmitting small, infrequent data payloads. Uses Grant-Free Transmission — devices skip the 4-step RACH procedure, eliminating signaling overhead. Realized through cellular LPWAN standards: NB-IoT and LTE-M (3GPP Rel-13, integrated into 5G from Rel-15).
⚡CONNECTION DENSITY
Target: 1,000,000 devices per km². Unlike eMBB which maximizes throughput for one connection, mMTC maximizes the number of simultaneous connections.
📶GRANT-FREE TRANSMISSION
Devices transmit on pre-configured uplink resources without executing the 4-step RACH handshake, reducing signaling overhead by ~60%.
💰LPWAN STANDARDS
NB-IoT and LTE-M are the cellular LPWAN technologies that realize mMTC — operating on licensed spectrum with deep coverage and ultra-low power consumption.

02 / Technology I
NB-IoT — Narrowband IoT
Standardized in 3GPP Release 13, NB-IoT operates on 180 kHz bandwidth — one LTE Resource Block — within licensed spectrum. It supports three deployment modes: In-Band, Guard-Band, and Standalone. Its narrow spectral focus produces a Power Spectral Density gain achieving 164 dB MCL, a +20 dB improvement over GPRS, enabling signal penetration into underground and deep-indoor environments unreachable by standard LTE.

03 / Technology II
LTE-M — Long Term Evolution for Machines
Standardized in 3GPP Release 13
1.4 MHz Bandwidth
Wider channel than NB-IoT, supporting peak data rates up to 1 Mbps — sufficient for real-time health monitoring and live asset location streaming.
Full Handover Support
Seamless tower-to-tower session continuity as a device crosses cell boundaries. Essential for logistics trackers, cold-chain containers, and fleet management.
VoLTE & FOTA Support
Voice over LTE enables emergency SOS calls from wearables. FOTA (Firmware Over-The-Air) support allows remote firmware updates without physical device access.
156 dB MCL
Maximum Coupling Loss of 156 dB — strong coverage for outdoor and semi-indoor deployments across wide geographic areas.

06 / Massive Access Control
Managing 10⁶ Simultaneous Connections
PSM — Power Saving Mode
Device negotiates a sleep timer (T3412) with the network post-transmission. Radio shuts down completely, drawing only 5–15 μA. Remains network-attached — no re-authentication on wake-up. For a sensor reporting once per hour, the radio is active for ~0.5 seconds out of every 3,600.
eDRX — Extended Discontinuous Reception
Extends paging listening interval from LTE's 1.28 seconds to up to 43.69 minutes. Device wakes, checks for a page, and returns to idle — consuming almost nothing. This is how a device stays reachable without staying awake.

07 / Security Framework
Lightweight Security for Resource-Constrained Devices
Security in mMTC must be cryptographically robust yet executable on microcontroller-class hardware. 5G addresses this through three purpose-designed mechanisms:
SUCI — Subscription Concealed Identifier
Device identity encrypted before transmission. Eliminates IMSI-catching attacks present in earlier generations by ensuring the permanent identifier is never sent in cleartext.
5G-AKA — Authentication and Key Agreement
Mutual authentication protocol optimized for low signaling overhead. Prevents man-in-the-middle attacks without exhausting the constrained device CPU.
AES-128 / Snow-3G
Lightweight symmetric cipher suites providing data integrity protection — critical for smart grid commands and industrial control signals where tampered data has physical consequences.
